A Constraints

Structural facts that change how the work can be done. None of these are bugs; all of them constrain the sequence.

fact Production schema is ahead of git

Migration 0028 (the be_outcome enum) is applied in production, but its .sql, its snapshot, and the schema.ts change are all uncommitted. A reset from a clean checkout would rebuild a different schema. This is why Phase 0 exists.

ledger newest timestamp 1784286029059 matches _journal.json entry 28; git status lists 0028_many_black_panther.sql as untracked

fact The migrations ledger survives a public-schema truncate

The Drizzle ledger lives in its own drizzle schema, not public. Truncating all 67 public tables cannot break db:migrate. Note that db:push is hard-blocked in this repo after the 2026-06-30 ledger incident.

drizzle.__drizzle_migrations, 28 rows; pg_tables shows 1 drizzle-schema table, 67 in public

fact seed/cleanup.ts is not a reset

It deletes from 22 tables. 44 tables hold data. Running pnpm db:seed on its own would leave roughly 14,700 rows behind, dominated by asset_pivots, and would look like it worked. The reset needs its own truncate.

exact count(*) across all 67 tables: 18,000 total rows; cleanup covers trades, trade_*, strategies, accounts, users, and 13 more, but not asset_pivots (13,790), monthly_tax_ledger, the four *_plan tables, or both account_*_aggregate tables

fact tags has no dimension column

The only classification axis is type, an enum of setup / mistake / general, plus a free-form color. A multi-dimensional tag model has to encode the dimension in the name (exec:virada-de-box, nivel:media-1) or the schema needs a new column. See decision B1.

src/db/schema.ts, tags table: name varchar(50), type enum, unique on (userId, name)

fact risk_management_profiles holds no numbers

Every numeric column was dropped in Phase 4b. The table now carries a single untyped decisionTree text blob, and the real caps moved to the fractal cascade in R-multiples (yearlyPlans.defaultDailyLossR and friends). Overlay §18.9 is denominated in reais against a shrinking loss buffer, so it does not map onto R caps without a decision. See B4.

B Decisions

All five answered by Ygor on 2026-09-01. Recorded here with the consequence each one carries, and merged into playbook.md §18.

B1 Tag dimensions ride on a name prefix

No migration. Four axes encoded by convention in tags.name: exec: for execution mode, nivel: for the level entered, tf: for the level’s timeframe, risco: for the derived matrix cell. The longest generated name is well inside varchar(50), and prefix queries need no schema support.

Cost accepted: the prefix is a convention, so nothing in the database enforces it. A typo creates a silent fifth axis. Seeder owns the vocabulary.

B2 figura, dobra and SDVB seed as inactive

Seeded with isActive=false rather than omitted, so doctrine stays complete and historical references resolve. The overlay is the filter, not the record.

26 of 47 codes inactive after §18.10 landed. Active set is 21.

B3 Stop is the 5min always, and risk 8–10 is accepted

No exception for 60min-level entries. The matrix cost is taken as written: a 60min-level entry paying a 5min stop is risk 8 on a virada and 10 on a teste, which are its worst cells, on the setup §5A calls premium. The decision does not resolve that conflict, it picks a side of it.

What makes it defensible: with the stop timeframe fixed, entry risk becomes a function of the level alone and collapses to six values — 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10. That is a single clean analysis axis. The risco: tag turns the argument into a measurement: whether risk 8 and 10 entries actually pay is answerable from the journal after a block of trades, instead of debatable from doctrine.

Merged into playbook.md §18.1 with the matrix table inline.

B4 R against a re-anchored buffer — and the rule itself was wrong

Two things came out of implementing this, and the second one matters more.

Axion has two R fields with different denominators. realizedRMultiple divides P&L by the position’s own planned risk, and positionSize cancels top and bottom, so a full stop reads −1R on 1 contract or on 10. It is blind to sizing by construction. rOutcome divides P&L by oneRCents, a fixed money amount from the capital-ladder tier that never sees the contract count. The circuit breaker uses the second one. Summing the first one to check a daily limit is the 2× error this check existed to find.

Calibration decided: oneRCents = the stop of one contract = 2 boxes × R of the 5min × R$1,00. At this week’s 22R that is 4400. Chosen over the two-contract stop because oneRCents is contract-independent by design; anchored on one contract, rOutcome always reads “how many contract-stops did I lose” and survives every change of hand and of R.

Revised same day: the hand is 1 contract, not 2. Ygor moved to Pedro’s rule, whose spreadsheet gives 1 contract for a R$4k budget. So defaultDailyLossR = 3, from floor((4000 ÷ 30) ÷ 44) = floor(3,03). Cross-check: 3 losses × 1 contract × R$44 = R$132 against an allowance of R$133,33.

This is where anchoring on one contract paid off. Halving the hand did not touch oneRCents — it is still 4400, because one contract’s stop did not change. Only defaultDailyLossR moved, 6 to 3. Anchored on the position stop instead (R$88), the same change would have forced a rewrite of oneRCents, invalidated the frozen oneRSnapshotCents on every prior trade, and made no historical rOutcome comparable to a new one. One-line change instead of a migration.

The 2x hazard is now dormant, not gone. At 1 contract both R fields read −1R on a full stop, so any reconciliation check run today passes. The divergence reappears on the first day the hand goes to 2. Latent bug, not absent bug.

At 1 contract the lever stops being contracts. There is no half contract, so the countdown resizing rule has nowhere to go. With the allowance fixed at R$133,33, what a high-R week breaks is the number of losses that fit. Checked against all 134 measured weeks:

The §18.9 rule as published yesterday was broken — found while implementing this, and worth keeping on the record even though 1 contract makes the corrected version inert (there is nothing to resize below the floor). It said allowance = remaining buffer ÷ 15, which reapplies the divisor every day to a buffer that already shrank, demanding 15 fresh days of runway daily. Simulated across all five R levels it does the opposite of what I claimed: it halts earlier than a fixed hand and leaves R$1.228 to R$2.980 unspent. At 34R it quits on day 5 holding 74% of the buffer.

Corrected rule: days remaining counts down from 15, allowance = remaining buffer ÷ days remaining, and the hand never increases inside a cycle. The trailing clause is what separates the last two rows — without it, days-remaining reaches 1 and the allowance becomes the whole buffer in one session.

Read the table as history, not reference. Every day count above is computed on the superseded 15-day divisor. Under Pedro’s 30 days the same arithmetic gives 1 contract at 14R, 17R and 22R and zero at 26R and above, which is exactly why the lever moved to the loss count. Do not carry these figures forward if the hand ever rises.

The trigger is the week’s R, not yesterday’s loss. Also corrected: I wrote that one full-stop day drops him to 1 contract. It does not. Losing exactly the allowance holds the ratio constant and the hand stays at 2 indefinitely, which is the point of amortizing. What halves the hand is R rising: 3 contracts at 14R, 2 at 17R and 22R, 1 at 26R and above. His 2-contract decision is right this week and falls out on its own past 25R.

Merged as playbook.md §18.11, with §18.9 rewritten and the error recorded in place. Ladder must be indexed on the remaining buffer, not on capital, because the T2 R$4k is a loss limit.

model Seven families, four tag axes

strategies holds the seven real families. Everything else becomes a tag dimension: execution (teste, virada-de-box, rompimento, leilao), level (14 values), level timeframe (5 / 15 / 60min), and risk cell, which should be derived at entry from level timeframe plus stop timeframe plus execution rather than stored, so the app can flag an 8 or a 10 as it is entered.

The analytical payoff is the argument. With 47 strategies, “is my teste worse than my virada de box?” is unanswerable because each code carries two or three trades. With seven families plus an execution tag, that question runs across the whole sample.

Locked by B1 and B3. Axes are name prefixes, no migration: exec:, nivel:, tf:, risco:. Because B3 fixes the stop at 5min for every strategy, risco: is fully determined by the level timeframe and the execution, so it collapses to six values — risco:1, risco:2, risco:4, risco:5, risco:8, risco:10. Derived at entry, not stored, so the app can flag an 8 or a 10 while it is being typed.

The cut costs less than it looks. Pedro calibrates the moving averages against Fibonacci deliberately, and the vault records the consequence: a pullback to the 60min mean lands on roughly the 61.8% retracement. So the media family already trades that region under another name. Dropping 38.2 and 50 removes the shallow levels, not the good one; most of the 7-to-2 collapse in fibonacci is deduplication.

trap The codes cannot be parsed, only mapped

Two reasons. T is overloaded: in TPA and TLTA it means teste, but in TA, TD and TS it means triângulo. And prefixes stack, so DTPA is dobra plus teste plus price-action, three levels deep. The seed must carry an explicit mapping table.

naive prefix-stripping failed on 6 of 47 codes: AJUSTE, DERIVA, TA, TD, TS, DTPA

E Sequence

Each phase blocks the next, so the order carries information. Phases 1, 2 and 4 are non-destructive and ready. Phase 3 is the only one that needs an explicit yes, and nothing after it runs until it has one.

  1. ready Settle the schema

    Commit 0028 with its snapshot, _journal.json and the schema.ts change, plus the other modified files. No database writes. Constraint A1 is why this is first.

  2. ready Snapshot

    pg_dump to a gzipped file outside the repo. About 18,000 rows, so seconds. Read-only, and it gives back the real fills to reference when the live account is re-seeded.

  3. needs go Truncate

    One statement across all 67 public tables with RESTART IDENTITY CASCADE, so foreign-key cycles resolve themselves. Then pnpm db:migrate to confirm the ledger is intact. Dry run is built and the statement is printed; this is the step that needs an explicit yes.

  4. ready Rewrite the seeders

    Unblocked: every B and C decision is answered. Reference seeders stay (assets, timeframes, settings, fee rates). Demo personas go. Concrete target:

    • Identity: one admin user, one default account, mode=hawks. Nothing else. No backtest account, no second live account.
    • Strategies: 47 codes from the explicit mapping table, 21 isActive=true and 26 false, grouped into 7 families.
    • Tags: the 16 stale rows are not carried over. New taxonomy on the four prefix axes, owned by the seeded admin.
    • Renko: 268 measured rows replace the 22 synthetic ones.
    • OCO: derived per asset per week from size5m, both WIN and WDO, no hardcoded dollar row.
    • Risk: oneRCents=4400 and defaultDailyLossR=3 for the current week (1 contract, 3 losses), ladder indexed on remaining buffer. Weekly and monthly caps left unset pending Ygor’s call.
    • Scenarios: rebuilt from Hawks periods only; HWK_S07/S08/S09 dropped, HWK_S15 corrected.

  5. blocked on E3 Verify

    Row counts per table against the intended seed, every enum value checked against the schema, and the derived OCO numbers recomputed from the Renko series rather than trusted.
