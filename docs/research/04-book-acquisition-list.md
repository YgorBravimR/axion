# Subject 1 — Market Structure

## Top picks (must-read tier)

| #   | Book                                | Author / Year                   | Why it matters                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Mind Over Markets**               | Jim Dalton, 1997                | Steidlmayer teaches Profile _layout_; Dalton teaches market _logic_ through Profile. The operational bridge — TPO clustering, value-area logic, why smart money bids/offers where it does. Closest textbook to the Hawks reading framework. |
| 2   | **Trading and Exchanges**           | Larry Harris, 2003              | The academic foundation of market microstructure — order types, spreads, adverse selection, price discovery mechanism. Fills the _causation_ gap his library lacks. Harris balances rigor + accessibility better than Hasbrouck.            |
| 3   | **Markets in Profile**              | Jim Dalton, 2011                | Multi-timeframe Profile reading — accumulation → markup → distribution. Maps 1:1 to Hawks TOPO/FUNDO detection. Companion to #1.                                                                                                            |
| 4   | **The Art of Tape Reading**         | Humphrey B. Neill, 1931 (repr.) | THE canonical source for modern order-flow practitioners. Reading bid/ask imbalance, absorption, panic. Forces reading _dynamics_, not candles. Reads as relevant today as when B3 was a pit.                                               |
| 5   | **The Wyckoff Method**              | David Weis (modern ed., 2022)   | Wyckoff's institutional scheme (accumulation/markup/distribution) is the textbook model for what "smart money" means before SMC repackaged it. Weis is the most readable modern treatment.                                                  |
| 6   | **Empirical Market Microstructure** | Joel Hasbrouck, 2007            | If Harris is the survey, Hasbrouck is the empirical methods — spread decomposition, Kyle's lambda, realized variance. Validates "order flow matters" at statistical-inference level. **Technical**: read selectively.                       |

## High-value second tier

7. **Pit Bull** — Marty Schwartz, 2012 — Discretionary pit-trader memoir. Honest about losses. One-tick stop discipline + contrarian reads. Single-sitting read.
8. **Masters in the Markets** + **Master the Markets** — Tom Williams, 2012/2019 — Wyckoff systematized for modern bars/charts. Concrete pattern library.
9. **Technical Analysis Explained** — Martin Pring, 5th ed. 2014 — Broad TA reference. Lower novelty, high reference value.
10. **The Art of Stock Market Speculation** — Jesse Stine, 1909 (repr.) — Pure price-action discipline. Predates indicators by 80 years.

## Worth knowing exist (read once, cite, don't buy)

- **Kyle "Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading"** (1985, academic) — the Kyle's Lambda paper. Cited everywhere; Harris explains the intuition.
- **Profile Trading** — Semelhago, 2005 — lighter than Dalton.
- **The Logical Trader** — Mark Fisher, 2002 — ATR breakouts; formulaic.

## Skip despite hype

- **Mike Bellafiore** (One Good Trade / Trading Therapy) — course-funnel; motivational; light on mechanics.
- **"SMC textbook" anthologies** on Amazon — ICT is video-only; printed transcripts are middleman extractions. Watch original ICT directly; the conceptual content reduces to Auction + Wyckoff.
- **Bookmap "Complete Guide"** — UI walkthrough, not methodology. Use their free academy videos.
- **Ryan Jones — The Trading Game** — subsumed by Vince/Tharp.
- **Linda Raschke — Street Smarts** — overlaps Schwartz; less rigorous.

## Brazilian-specific gap

The literature gap is **NOT** content — the canon is read. The gap is **domain specificity**: Renko calibration on índice/dólar (Hawks: 50R-101R 5m / 12R-17R 15m regime-dependent), WDO microstructure (bank flow, BC signals, carry-trade reversals), B3 liquidity windows (US open + Brazilian afternoon + leilão de fechamento). **No book covers this.** Your Hawks engine + Axion playbook ARE the reference.

# Subject 2 — Money Management

## Top picks (must-read tier)

| #   | Book                            | Author / Year                 | Why beyond Tharp/Vince                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **A Man for All Markets**       | Ed Thorp, 2015                | Kelly's co-inventor's memoir. Applied Kelly at hedge-fund scale: when to fractional-Kelly, when institutional sizing breaks. Intuition + blowup stories that Tharp's textbook doesn't deliver. |
| 2   | **Trading Systems and Methods** | Perry Kaufman, 2013 (4th ed.) | Deeper risk-of-ruin Monte Carlo + sequence-dependency + leverage compounding than Vince or Tharp. Encyclopedic — focus on Ch. 7 and Ch. 11.                                                    |
| 3   | **Leveraged Trading**           | Robert Carver, 2020           | Bridges per-trade rules (2%/6%) → portfolio-level (volatility targeting, correlation regimes, rebalancing under leverage). Single trader, multiple instruments.                                |
| 4   | **Fortune's Formula**           | William Poundstone, 2005      | Narrative of Kelly criterion's discovery and failure modes. _Why_ fractional Kelly survives where full Kelly blows up. Philosophical grounding behind the math.                                |
| 5   | **Red-Blooded Risk**            | Aaron Brown, 2011             | Risk manager's perspective. _Why_ institutions chose specific sizing rules. Failure-mode reasoning for rule customization.                                                                     |
| 6   | **Skin in the Game**            | Nassim Taleb, 2018            | Extends _Antifrágil_ with the alignment principle: your capital, your rules. 90% of blowups are misaligned incentives. _Skip if Antifrágil is fresh in mind._                                  |

## High-value second tier

7. **Systematic Trading** + **Smart Portfolios** — Robert Carver — companions to _Leveraged Trading_, less essential individually.
8. **The Most Important Thing** — Howard Marks, 2011 — Marks's risk philosophy (you have _Dominando o Ciclo de Mercado_; this is the prequel).
9. **Asset Management** — Andrew Ang, 2014 — academic portfolio construction; reference, not read-through.

## Worth knowing exist (read-once, cite, don't buy)

- **Fooled by Randomness** — Taleb, 2001 — redundant with _Antifrágil_.
- **The Intelligent Investor** — Graham, classic — wrong audience for intraday futures.
- **A Random Walk Down Wall Street** — Malkiel — EMH conflicts with edge assumption.

## Skip despite hype

- **Rich Dad Poor Dad** — motivational filler.
- **Against the Gods** — Bernstein — risk history; not practice-applicable to intraday.

## Critical gaps (no book solves these)

1. **Drawdown recovery psychology** — canonical gap. Build your own postMorten library.
2. **Equity-curve trading / strategy rotation** — Tharp has one chapter; no rigorous text exists.
3. **B3-specific leverage & margin-call mechanics** — broker rules trump theory.
4. **Portuguese money-management canon** — doesn't exist. Don't search.

# Subject 3 — Trader Psychology

## Top picks (must-read tier)

| #   | Book                        | Author / Year                       | What it adds beyond Douglas/Tendler                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Thinking in Bets**        | Annie Duke, 2018                    | Better-evidenced version of Douglas's probabilistic framework. Poker-pro → behavioral-econ translator. Decouples decision quality from outcome quality more rigorously than Tendler.                    |
| 2   | **Sources of Power**        | Gary Klein, 1998 (20th anniv. 2017) | Recognition-Primed Decision Model. The _rigorous_ version of Gladwell's _Blink_ — Klein is the primary source. Maps to Hawks pattern-recognition under time pressure.                                   |
| 3   | **Superforecasting**        | Phil Tetlock + Dan Gardner, 2015    | Empirical proof (Good Judgment Project) that calibration is trainable. Codifies confidence calibration + base-rate reasoning + Bayesian belief updates. Complements Duke with peer-reviewed validation. |
| 4   | **The Happiness Trap**      | Russ Harris, 2007                   | Self-help-grade ACT (Acceptance & Commitment Therapy). Per deep-research finding: ACT > CBT for tilt. Reframes emotion regulation from suppression to values-aligned acceptance.                        |
| 5   | **The Checklist Manifesto** | Atul Gawande, 2009                  | Aviation/surgical discipline → scaled. Not "psychology" per se but operational implementation of process-over-outcome. Direct parallel: pre-trade / management / post-trade checklists.                 |
| 6   | **Trading Psychology 2.0**  | Brett Steenbarger, 2015             | The Steenbarger synthesis (he has 6+ books; this is the one). Process optimization framing. 20% new, 80% reinforcement — but the 20% is high-value. Skip _Daily Trading Coach_ in favor of this.        |

## High-value second tier

7. **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Kahneman, 2011 — System 1/2 dichotomy + anchoring + representativeness. You have Ariely/Dobelli; this is the foundation. Optional if bandwidth-constrained.
8. **Deep Work** — Cal Newport, 2016 — attention as a finite resource. Better than Goleman's _Foco_ for operational application. Directly applies to pre-market routine + session focus.
9. **Market Mind Games** — Denise Shull, 2012 — interoception framework. Real ideas, verbose book. Read a Shull podcast first.

## Worth knowing exist (read once, cite, don't buy)

- **Complications** — Gawande, 2002 — pre-_Checklist_ book on systemic medical error.
- **Never Split the Difference** — Chris Voss, 2016 — FBI negotiator. Tangential to trading.
- **ACT (academic)** — Steven Hayes — the textbook. Harris's _Happiness Trap_ is enough.

## Skip despite hype

- **The Psychology of Trading** — Douglas (pre-_Trading in the Zone_) — earlier, less polished.
- **The Daily Trading Coach** — Steenbarger — 101 atomized lessons. _Psychology 2.0_ is his stronger work.
- **Blink** — Gladwell — read Klein's _Sources of Power_ instead.
- **The Art of Impossible** — Kotler — flow-science marketing; less actionable than Newport.
- **Fooled by Randomness** — Taleb — redundant with _Antifrágil_.

## Specific gaps where NO good book exists

1. **B3 index-futures discretionary psychology** — generic day-trading literature only. Build from your own logs.
2. **Interoception for traders (peer-reviewed)** — read the neuroscience papers directly, not Shull's book.
3. **ACT specifically applied to tilt / variance** — Harris is general. Map it yourself.
4. **Portuguese trader psychology (serious)** — doesn't exist academically.
5. **Naturalistic decision-making for discretionary futures traders** — Klein is closest but studies firefighters / nurses.

# Reading order for the Tier-S books (when they arrive)

1. **Gawande — _Checklist Manifesto_** (2 evenings) — installs the operational frame. Read first.
2. **Duke — _Thinking in Bets_** (1 week) — recalibrates your probabilistic vocabulary.
3. **Klein — _Sources of Power_** (2 weeks) — reframes when trained intuition is allowed.
4. **Dalton — _Mind Over Markets_** (3 weeks) — install the auction-theory operating system.
5. **Thorp — _A Man for All Markets_** (1 week, casual) — read on weekends; intuition over math.

Then re-read your existing canon. You'll see things you missed the first time because these new lenses change what's salient.
